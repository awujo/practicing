Module 0 · Orientation

The Storyline Method & Trade Structure

Before any technique: how Kevin thinks about trading. Everything is a storyline read off higher-timeframe structure, and every trade plan splits into WHEN and WHERE.

Process

The Storyline: "When" and "Where"

▶

Kevin divides his entire trading process into two aspects:

  • WHEN — tells him when to look for a setup. It is complete only when the full chain of criteria is met: basic market structure → advanced market structure → liquidity → key levels → confirmed storyline.
  • WHERE — tells him where to enter the trade from. If he cannot define a good entry place, he has no valid stop-loss location either — so he leaves the trade alone.
A clear storyline must exist (e.g. weekly reject weekly + daily breakout). A direction being "clear" is not yet a setup — it only says WHEN you may hunt.
Once the storyline is confirmed, wait for the entry model to present itself. Some storylines come with an immediate entry; others require patience.
No entry? No trade. "The chart is never going to run" — if today's storyline or entry is unclear, tomorrow will bring another one. Forcing trades is how accounts die.
⚠️ "The market is never wrong, but you will always see what you want to see. In a bearish market, if you are looking for a buy, the market will show you a buy." A written structure protects you from yourself.
💡 One clean TP per week with strict criteria is enough to be profitable. Getting only a few setups in three weeks is an improvement, not a problem — the issue is usually capital/time pressure, not the strategy.

Session: "Structuring Your Trades" + Q&A #1

Rule

Timeframe Stacking: Bias TF → Entry TF

▶

The golden rule of multi-timeframe analysis: the timeframe that confirms your storyline is the timeframe you start hunting entries on — one step below the bias timeframe.

Monthly story → weekly entries · Weekly story → daily entries — always the very next timeframe down (Daily story → H4, etc.)
  • Storyline can appear on any timeframe — but entries come from the very next timeframe that confirmed the break.
  • Higher-timeframe entries hold better: price respects weekly/daily POIs far more reliably than 15-minute ones.
  • Example: Daily Reject Daily + H4 breakout → hunt entries on H4; drop to H2/H1 only to refine (see Entry Module).

Module 1 · Foundations

Basic Market Structure

The skeleton of everything: swings, HH/HL, LL/LH, and the difference between a Break of Structure and a Change of Character. If you can't mark these cleanly on a line chart, nothing downstream works.

Definition

Swing Points — mark the extremes of reversals

▶

Structure starts with swing points. The rule Kevin drills: mark the highest point before price reversed downward, and the lowest point before price reversed upward.

  • A high only exists where price reversed to the downside; a low only where price reversed to the upside.
  • Tiny pullbacks inside a strong push do not count as swings in basic structure — only the extreme point that preceded the reversal.
  • Pro-trick: switch to the line chart — noise disappears and the true swing points stand out. Verify every marking there.

Session: "Basic Market Structure"

Definition

BOS vs BMS (CHoCH)

▶
  • BOS — Break of Structure: price continues with the trend, breaking the last swing in the trend's direction (in an uptrend: breaks above the last HH; in a downtrend: breaks below the last LL). Confirms the trend is intact.
  • BMS — Break of Market Structure (Kevin's term for CHoCH / Change of Character): price breaks structure against the trend — e.g. in an uptrend, a body closes below the last Higher Low. Signals direction is about to change.
📌 Both require a full candle BODY close past the level. A wick poking through is NOT a break — it's a sweep (a liquidity event). This single distinction powers half the course.

Sessions: "Basic Market Structure", "Advanced Market Structure Pt 1–3"

Rule

Reading the Three Market Phases

▶
  • Uptrend: continuous HH + HL until a body closes below the last HL (BMS).
  • Downtrend: continuous LL + LH until a body closes above the last Lower High.
  • Ranging: neither — price chops between equal highs/lows. In a range, external liquidity builds on both sides; expect sweeps, respect few internal levels, and reduce expectations of clean AMS legs.
💡 "The ability to spot the trend on the higher timeframe is the ONLY thing you need in this market-structure method." Everything else is decoration.

Sessions: "Key Levels Pt 1" (recap), "Basic Market Structure"

Definition

AI / IH — Internal Breaks & Inducement

▶

Inside a trend leg, minor pullback levels get broken internally — Kevin labels these AI (internal break of a low-side level) and IH (internal break of a high-side level). They are mini-BOS events inside the leg, not structural changes.

  • An inducement (IDM) is a level created after a BOS that lures early traders in — a fake sell inside a bullish story, a fake buy inside a bearish story.
  • Inducement almost always sits at the last AI/IH before the real move resumes — institutions use it to grab opposite-side orders before expanding toward the real target.
  • "Inducement usually breaks AI" — the pullback that takes out the inducement is often exactly the retracement that gives you your entry.
🧠 Why it exists: institutions must buy cheap / sell expensive. They trick early sellers/buyers into entering, sweep their stops, then move in the real direction. The inducement's job is to trap the impatient — so let price sweep it before you commit.

Module 2 · Intermediate

Advanced Market Structure (AMS)

One leg on a higher timeframe contains a whole story on a lower one. AMS = Sweep → BOS → Inducement, repeated. Learn to see the fractal and you'll know where pullbacks are coming.

Definition

The Sweep (Liquidity Grab)

▶

A sweep is when price takes out a swing point with a wick only and closes back on the original side. On the higher TF it looks like a tiny pin bar; zoom into H4/H1 and you'll find a full sweep + break sequence inside that single candle.

  • Wick clears the previous swing's wick, body closes back → sweep (wick-to-wick).
  • Body closes beyond the previous swing → it was not a sweep, it's a BOS.
  • Every swing high/low has resting orders above/below it — sweeping it means those orders were collected as fuel.

Sessions: "Advanced Market Structure Pt 1", "AMS Pt 3"

Rule

The AMS Formula: Sweep → BOS → Inducement

▶

An advanced market structure leg consists of exactly three ingredients, in order:

Sweep of liquidity (grab of the last swing with a wick).
BOS in the new direction — sometimes a double BOS (first break confirms intent, second break after a fresh internal level confirms momentum).
Inducement level created (the AI/IH just before expansion).
Price retraces to sweep the inducement — this retracement is your entry window — then expands toward the external target.
After each BOS, price pulls back to sweep the inducement before continuing. That pullback = entry opportunity at a key level.
🔁 Fractal truth: one clean leg on the daily contains this entire AMS picture on H4/H1. When you see a sweep+BOS candle stack on the HTF, expect the completed AMS sequence underneath it.

Sessions: "Advanced Market Structure Pt 1", "AMS Pt 3", "Key Levels Pt 1" (USDJPY example)

Rule

Validating the Break — Kevin's Strict Standard

▶
  • A BOS is validated only when a full body closes beyond the last point marked as the AI/IH (the relevant swing) — never on a wick.
  • Kevin waits for the external/structural break even if it's far away, rather than trading early internal breaks. Internal breakouts are continuations, not direction changes.
  • Some traders label an early ChoCH and act on it; Kevin deliberately uses the later, cleaner break — fewer traps, slightly later entries.
  • After the sweep, the next body that closes beyond the swept swing's level is the confirming BOS.
🧪 Assignment habit: mark AMS sequences on the line chart first (sweep, BOS, IDM), then overlay candles. Most assignment errors came from marking on candle noise instead of line-chart truth.

Sessions: "AMS Pt 1", "AMS Pt 3", "Entry Part 4" (internal vs external break)

Worked Example

Classic USDJPY Leg — WTW Sweep → BOS → IDM → Pullback Entry

▶
Daily uptrend intact. Price makes a wick-to-wick sweep of a prior high area (no body close above → treated as liquidity, not a break).
Follow-through BOS above the swing — trend continuation confirmed.
The lowest point that pushed price into the break becomes the inducement (it broke the last AI on the way up).
Price returns into that level, reacts from it, and rides a large bullish move — a textbook AMS pullback entry. Because it was a wick-to-wick (not a body break), price travelled with the trend much further.
📐 Contrast: had the same swing been broken with a body, we'd expect only a shallow visit to the swept low before structure failed. Wick-sweep = fuel for continuation; body-break = change of state.

Module 3 · Fuel

Liquidity — IRL & ERL

"Liquidity simply means money." Price travels toward wherever the money sits, and alternates between internal and external pools. This is what tells you targets and entry zones.

Definition

IRL vs ERL — the Two Types of Liquidity

▶
  • IRL — Internal Range Liquidity: money resting inside the current range/structure — the BOS levels, AI/IH points, inducements within the trend.
  • ERL — External Range Liquidity: money outside the current range — old swing highs/lows, previous day/week highs & lows, untouched equal highs/lows.
🎯 Practical use: after an AMS gives you an entry idea, your take-profit goes at the ERL. Price may blow straight through it, but the internal→external rotation is the baseline expectation.

Session: "Liquidity — IRL/ERL"

Rule

Structural Point vs Liquidity Point

▶

The same swing level can play two roles — the market tells you which one by how it treats it:

  • Structural point: price closes with a body beyond the last ILQ/level → it produced a BMS/BOS. The level did structure duty.
  • Liquidity point: price only wicks through it and closes back → the level was consumed as money. Expect a reversal/expansion in the other direction.
In an uptrend, the series of HLs keeps producing BOSes until one gets body-closed below. If instead it's merely wicked, that HL is now "treated as liquidity" — the sweep that fuels the next leg higher.

Session: "Liquidity — IRL/ERL" (bridge from basic → advanced structure)

Definition

Forms of Liquidity — Single-Candle Liquidity

▶

The two types (IRL/ERL) appear on the chart in many forms. One of them is single-candle liquidity: a lone candle whose high/low serves as a liquidity pool — often simultaneously acting as internal and standard-range liquidity depending on context.

  • Equal highs/lows (EQH/EQL), previous-day high/low, and round numbers are the other common forms we touched on.
  • Single-candle liquidity is frequently the magnet right before an expansion: price pops its high/low, grabs the resting orders, then moves.
📚 Kevin flagged this as the first form taught, with more forms assigned for self-study — treat "single candle liquidity" as the starter and keep a journal page for new forms as you meet them live.

Module 4 · Locations

Key Levels

Key levels are the zones where decisions get made — where you place bids and offers. Master the three primitives (A-shape, V-shape, Open-Close) and their freshness rules, and every complex POI in the course unfolds from them.

Definition

What Is a Key Level?

▶

A key level is a level/area/zone on the chart where we expect price to react whenever it taps in — either a buy or a sell reaction. They are not random lines: they are zones where decisions are made.

  • Rejection (= failure): price hits the level and is forcefully pushed away — the reaction we wanted.
  • Acceptance / impulsive disrespect: price closes through it with full bodies — the level flips roles (see invalidation below).

Session: "Key Levels Pt 1"

Definition

The Three Primitives — A-shape, V-shape, Open-Close

▶

All key levels evolve from three basic formations:

  • A-shape: two candles of opposite direction — bullish first, then bearish — sharing the same body level. Looks like capital "A" on the line chart. Used for SELLS.
  • V-shape: two candles of opposite direction — bearish first, then bullish. Looks like "V" on the line chart. Used for BUYS.
  • Open-Close (OC): two consecutive candles facing the same direction. Bullish OB (two bullish) → used for buys; Bearish OB (two bearish) → used for sells. Invisible on line charts — candles only.
🔍 The shared body level is what matters — wicks are irrelevant when locating the zone.

Sessions: "Key Levels Pt 1", "Key Levels Pt 3"

Rule

Invalidation & Role Flipping

▶

Expect a sell from an A-shape — but if price breaks above it impulsively (full body close), the level is invalidated and its role flips:

  • A-shape meant for sells, body-closed above → price is expected to return into it and BUY from it.
  • V-shape meant for buys, body-closed below → expect a return and a SELL from it.
  • And it can flip again: after serving the buy, an impulsive body close back below restores the sell role. One level can serve both directions over its life — always read the last reaction.
⚠️ The keyword is impulsive. A grinding, wicky poke through ≠ invalidation. A decisive full-body close through = "price has accepted that level".

Session: "Key Levels Pt 3"

Rule

Fresh vs Unfresh Levels

▶
  • Fresh: created but never used — nothing has traded from or through it yet. Fresh levels carry more power.
  • Unfresh (used): a WICK has traded into/from it. Wicks consume the level.
  • Crucial nuance: a full body trading through the level does not make it unfresh — bodies don't "use" a level, they invalidate or accept it. Only wick contact marks a level used.
  • Unfresh → Fresh again: if after the wick tap a full body closes beyond the level (impulsive break), the level resets and becomes fresh/valid again for the opposite role.

Session: "Key Levels Pt 4"

Rule

Confluence & Choosing the Strongest Level

▶

Charts are covered in key levels — "they are everywhere." So filter ruthlessly:

  • Stack = strength: when several levels sit in the same zone (e.g. a V-shape + OC + a BOS level), that zone is the strongest candidate.
  • HTF beats LTF: a daily/H4 level outranks an M15 level; if your LTF entry conflicts with a deeper HTF level, expect price to reach the HTF one first.
  • Match the direction: in a bullish trend, prefer buy-side levels (V-shapes, bullish OCs, invalidated-and-flipped A-shapes); bearish logic mirrors this.
  • Recent-breakout levels > orphan levels: the level that actually triggered the confirming break carries the story.

Module 5 · Execution

Entries — Models, Strength Ranking & Risk

With storyline + liquidity + key levels done, entries finally arrive. Kevin's core philosophy: trade continuation, not reversal — use the most recent breakout level, demand the third-candle rejection, and rank every candidate by strength.

Principle

Continuation Over Reversal — Never Counter-Trade

▶
  • His entry models are breakout + retest continuations: once direction is confirmed, price comes back to the breakout level and continues. Continuations mean price usually moves quickly from the POI — deep reversals aren't expected.
  • One-sided trigger: with a bearish confirmed story, he ignores every bullish signal until the target is hit. Counter-trading is possible but bleeds accounts.
  • He avoids catching the first post-breakout move (often a reversal-shaped retracement of unknown depth). Instead he waits for the retracement to finish, then rides the subsequent continuation legs.
  • QMR levels are reversal patterns — powerful, but he uses them mainly as magnets/prioritisation tools, preferring continuation entries after alignment.
🏠 House analogy: structure = foundation, liquidity/key levels = walls, storyline = roof, entry = POP ceiling — you only do the ceiling once the roof exists. No storyline → no business discussing entries.

Sessions: "Introduction to Entries", "Entry Part 2–4", Q&A #1

Model

Entry Model A — Third-Candle Rejection (Breakout + Retest)

▶

The signature entry. Sequence on the entry timeframe:

Candles 1–2 form a key level (A/V-shape pair).
Candle 3 taps the level and gets rejected — this is the mandatory ingredient.
Candle 4 breaks structure in the story's direction (the breakout).
Candle 5 retests the level → entry. Stop beyond the invalidation, target at external liquidity.
🔎 Selection rule: among the series of BOS levels in a move, only the ones with a third-candle rejection are tradeable. All other breakout levels are skipped — they lack the fingerprint.

Sessions: "Entry Part 2", "Entry Part 3"

Model

Entry Model B — QMRO (Sweep + BOS Reversal Pattern)

▶

QMRO = a liquidity sweep followed by a BOS — visually a head-and-shoulders style pattern with three tradeable zones:

  • Apex / Head — the swept extreme (least preferred).
  • Shoulder — Kevin's favourite entry zone of the three.
  • Neck — the break level; also valid, especially with rejection.
  • QMRO with history: a QMRO zone that also shows a third-candle rejection — the strongest version of this model.
  • Because QMRO is a reversal pattern, the first move into it is expected (that's the flip). Prefer to wait for the retracement to hit the zone, then confirm with an H1 break before committing — that's alignment (see next card).
  • Priority: if a normal BOS level and a QMRO level sit near each other, price targets the QMRO level — expect the nearer weaker level to be blasted through.

Sessions: "Entry Part 4", "Introduction to Entries" (QMR mention), US30 case study

Rule

Strength Ranking — the A-Series vs B-Series Hierarchy

▶

Not all breakout levels are equal. From the entry classes, the ladder (weakest → strongest) is:

  1. A-series — plain breakout levels (BOS levels with no rejection fingerprint).
  2. Breakout level WITH third-candle rejection — the standard tradeable continuation level.
  3. QMRO level WITH third-candle rejection ("QMRO with history") — beats a plain breakout-with-rejection; price prioritises it.
  4. B-series — the stronger refined versions of the models (e.g. Type 2B referenced in Q&A). "The B series are stronger than the A series."
⚖️ Two entries at once? Always take the most recent breakout level — unless the older one is a B-series/QMRO grade while the new one is A-grade; then price will likely come to the stronger one first.

Sessions: "Entry Part 4" (ranking statement), Q&A #1 (A vs B series)

Procedure

Alignment Check — Dropping Down One TF to Confirm

▶

How to move from story to trigger without gambling:

Storyline confirmed on bias TF (e.g. Daily Reject Daily).
Breakout on the next TF (H4) confirms direction — now hunt H4 levels with third-candle rejection.
No rejection on the H4 level? Drop to H2 and look for the same fingerprint there. Found → trade it. Not found → no entry; leave the pair.
Weekly-story variant: if the daily breakout lacks a rejection model, wait for price to tap the level and print an H4 break — that means H4 aligns with daily, then take the H4 rejection level.
QMRO variant: daily reject daily + H4 QMRO → wait for an H1 break inside the zone. Daily+H4+H1 all pointing the same way = alignment = pullback probably finished.
✅ Alignment payoff: when timeframes stack, you'll rarely see internal breaks fighting your direction — price tends to move from the entry almost immediately. Missed entries (price never returns to the POI) are normal and prove the model's strength.

Sessions: "Entry Part 2", "Entry Part 3", "Entry Part 4"

Procedure

Stop-Loss Placement & Risk Rules

▶
  • Default = cover the invalidation zone (safest). The invalidation point is the far side of the level you entered from.
  • If the zone is too wide, use the rejection candle itself (the 3rd candle / doji-indecision candle near your POI) — price hardly invalidates those. Experience-gated, so earn it first.
  • Pip guidelines (Kevin's own defaults): USD majors ≈ 10–15 pips (most entries land at 5–10); JPY pairs ≈ 15–20 pips due to volatility. A setup needing more than ~15 pips on USD majors is often skipped.
  • Big names (Tariq-style) use wide stops because they cover the whole invalidation zone — copying their sizing without their accuracy is how beginners blow up.
  • SLs getting tripped then price going your way? Just adjust — that's feedback, not fate. Never fix a stop by widening blindly; fix the level choice.
🛑 Don't be "selfish with your stop": if unsure, cover the invalidation zone. A tight stop on a correct level is earned through chart experience, not demanded on day one.

Session: Q&A #1 (stop-loss segment), "Entry Part 3" (invalidation via rejection candle)

Rule

Targets — Aim at External Liquidity

▶
  • TP goes at the ERL: old swing highs/lows, previous day/week extremes — the money outside the range.
  • Price may slice through the ERL and keep going (especially after a wick-sweep with trend); the ERL is still the default objective.
  • Internal liquidity taken + expansion underway → expect a reversal-pullback after ERL is tapped, then possibly rotation back to IRL. Plan partials around that rhythm.

Sessions: "Liquidity", "Entry Part 2–4"

Worked Example

Full Flow — Daily Reject Daily → H4 Retest Entry

▶
Daily: price rejects a daily key level (say a fresh V-shape after sweeping a low). BMS printed against the old downtrend → bullish storyline forming.
H4: wait for the breakout above the last H4 supply level (body close). That break confirms the daily story — H4 is now the entry timeframe.
Scan H4 BOS levels for the third-candle-rejection fingerprint on the most recent breakout. Spot it? Mark the level + its invalidation.
No rejection on that H4 level? Drop to H2, find the same fingerprint, otherwise stand down.
Execute: limit/reaction entry at the retest, SL covering the invalidation (~10–15 pips USD pair), TP at the daily ERL (yesterday's/last week's high). One-sided trigger: ignore all sell signals until target.
Journalling: if price never returned to the POI and flew to TP — log it as a missed entry, evidence the model worked, not that you failed.

Glossary

Every acronym used across the 15 sessions. Use the search bar up top to scan the whole notebook.

BMS

Break of Market Structure — Kevin's term for CHoCH: a body close against the trend past the last HL (in uptrends) or LH (in downtrends). Signals a potential direction change.

BOS

Break of Structure — a body close with the trend past the last swing high/low, confirming continuation.

HH / HL / LL / LH

Higher High, Higher Low, Lower Low, Lower High — the four swing labels of basic market structure.

AI / IH

Internal breaks of a low-side / high-side swing level inside a leg. Their loss/gain defines inducements and validates BOS on lower timeframes.

IDM (Inducement)

A fake-looking level created after BOS that lures early traders; price sweeps it before expanding. Often the exact retracement entry zone.

IRL

Internal Range Liquidity — resting orders inside the current dealing range (BOS levels, inducements).

ERL

External Range Liquidity — resting orders outside the range (old highs/lows, PDH/PDL). Default take-profit magnet.

Sweep / Liquidity Grab

Wick-only raid of a swing level followed by a close back on the original side. Consumes resting orders; not a structural break.

WTW / WTB

Wick-To-Wick (sweep) vs Wick-To-Body (break). The shorthand for telling liquidity grabs from real structural breaks.

OC (Open-Close)

Key level of two consecutive same-direction candles. Bullish OC → buy zone; bearish OC → sell zone. Invisible on line charts.

QMRO

Sweep-of-liquidity + BOS reversal pattern with Apex/Head, Shoulder and Neck zones. "With history" = plus third-candle rejection = strongest.

DRD / WRW

Shorthand storylines: "Daily Reject Daily (+ H4 breakout)", "Weekly Reject Weekly (+ daily breakout)". Name your story by its HTF rejection + confirming break.

POI

Point of Interest — the specific key level you intend to trade from.

SL / TP / RR

Stop-loss, Take-profit, Risk:Reward. Defaults here: SL covers invalidation (10–15 pips USD, 15–20 JPY), TP at ERL.

CHoCH

Change of Character — the wider trading-world synonym for Kevin's BMS.

EQH / EQL

Equal Highs / Equal Lows — twin liquidity pools; classic external targets and sweep candidates.
