# Audit part 2: bias, key levels, targets

The three areas part 1 listed as not audited. Read against
`SLK_SPEC.md` sections 1, 3 and 7.

The most important entry here is the first one, and it is a correction
to my own work in this session rather than to the engine's.

---

## C1. A "major bug" I nearly filed, and why it is not one

`BiasCascade.check_flip()` implements condition (c) as **the absence of
a lower-timeframe confirmation in the OLD direction**. SLK_SPEC section
1 states condition 3 as **the presence of a breakout or break of
structure in the NEW direction**, and Bishop refuses a trade when it
never appears:

> *"We said [we] want to see a breakout or a break of structure. That
> did not happen. That never happened here."* (bias-1 t=1770-1785, BTC)

Those are different rules, and I confirmed empirically that the cascade
flips on an **empty** confirmation window. I was ready to record this as
the most consequential misinterpretation in the engine, since bias gates
everything downstream.

It is not, for two reasons, and both had to be checked rather than
assumed.

**First, the negative formulation is sourced.** video-16 specifies it
directly: *"the NEXT LOWER timeframe must ALSO fail to give any further
continuation confirmation (no breakout, no sweep+break) in the ORIGINAL
bias direction."* That video is about not reversing too eagerly — a
rule for when to ABANDON a bias, which is a different operation from
establishing one.

**Second, the positive requirement exists, elsewhere.**
`_aligned_timeframes()` requires Daily **and** H4 to each show their own
breakout or sweep+break **confirming the trade's direction** within
`ALIGNMENT_LOOKBACK`, and `run_backtest` refuses the entry otherwise.
That is bias-1's condition 3, enforced at entry time instead of at flip
time.

### This also answers a question that has been open since 2026-09-18

STRATEGY_VS_IMPLEMENTATION asked whether running both `bias_at()` and
`_aligned_timeframes()` was double-counting one rule. It is not. They
are two different taught rules:

| machine | rule | source |
|---|---|---|
| `BiasCascade` | do not flip while the lower timeframe still confirms the old direction | video-16 |
| `_aligned_timeframes` | do not enter unless the lower timeframes confirm this direction now | bias-1 t=390-405 |

Requiring both is correct. That open question can be closed.

## C2. The narrow version of C1 that IS real

With `BIAS_CONFIRMATION_WINDOW = 1` the window holds the single next
confirmation after the rejection candle, and the constant's own comment
states the intended rule: *"you look at the lower timeframe's MOST
RECENT breakout, and if it points the new way, the bias has flipped."*

That is a positive test in every case except one: **when the window is
empty, the code flips anyway.** No breakout is not a breakout pointing
the new way.

Scope is small and worth stating precisely rather than dramatising. The
window is empty only when the rejection candle falls after the last
confirmation in the whole series — i.e. at the tail of the data, not
mid-history. It cannot explain anything about historical results.

Left unfixed: changing it moves every recorded backtest number, and it
belongs in the taught-vs-shipped comparison rather than smuggled in
beside it.

## C3. The bias caveat is not implemented

SLK_SPEC 1, from bias-1 t=780-855: when the daily gives **no breakout**
but instead one or two daily candles **reject** off the same weekly
zone, that still counts — and condition 3 then descends, so the
breakout must appear on **H4** instead.

Bishop runs this path live on XAUUSD (bias-1 t=1875-1890) and EURUSD
(t=1305-1335).

The engine cannot take those trades. `_aligned_timeframes` requires
Daily to have its **own** confirmation, so a daily that only rejected is
never "aligned" and the entry is refused. The engine is stricter than
taught here, in a way that removes a class of setups Bishop takes rather
than adding bad ones.

Not a bug. A missing rule, and now a specific one rather than a vague
"the caveat isn't handled".

## C4. Key-level polarity flips are not tradeable for V and A shapes

bias-1 t=195-300 applies the disrespect rule to **all four** shapes: a
V-shape closed below becomes a sell level, an A-shape closed above
becomes a buy level, and the same for open-close.

`is_disrespected()` is applied to V and A levels inside
`check_flip()` — so the flip is recognised as a BIAS event. What does
not exist is the flipped V or A level becoming a level you then trade
from. `open_close_disrespect_entry()` does that for open-close only.

Already recorded in STRATEGY_VS_IMPLEMENTATION; restated here with the
distinction between the two uses made explicit, because "the flip is
implemented" is true of one and false of the other.

## C5. Targets: the ERL definition matches

`_unswept_period_extremes()` returns each completed period's own high or
low that no later period has exceeded, excluding the period `as_of_time`
falls inside. That is bias-2's *"previous weekly high/low"* exactly — a
period's own extreme, not a swing fractal — and the exclusion of the
still-forming period is right, since an unfinished period is not yet a
"previous" anything.

`_nearest_erl()` then takes the nearest such point beyond entry in the
trade direction, which is the taught *"first target"*. No divergence
found.
