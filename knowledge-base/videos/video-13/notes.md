## THE CONFIRMATION-ENTRY DECISION TREE (exhaustive, directly codeable)
After price takes out IRL and rejects from a key level on a higher timeframe, scale one timeframe down. What you see next falls into one of these branches:

**Branch 1 — Clean confirmation**: the lower-timeframe candle that taps the key level closes back through it (and often through the IRL too) -> strong confirmation -> expect direct continuation to the next key level.

**Branch 2 — Slow grind with fresh IRL**: two consecutive candles in the trend direction form a NEW open-close key level, a third candle taps the original IRL, then on an even-lower timeframe you see an engulfing that creates a new A/V-shape key level. The next candle after THAT engulfing serves as a fresh IRL that must be swept before continuing (i.e. IRL requirement propagates down a level).

**Branch 3 — Engulfing without a fresh IRL**: same engulfing as Branch 2, but price taps the new key level directly and continues, without a separate IRL forming first (matches Video 12's "IRL optional for continuation" — Branch 2 and 3 are the "necessary" vs "optional" split applied concretely).

**Branch 4 — Nested disrespected-open-close (quantified)**: instead of respecting the new key level, two consecutive candles form ANOTHER open-close that then gets **disrespected** (broken with an impulsive move). In this case: **~70% chance price does NOT return to the original V/A-shape "extreme" key level**, and instead returns to this newer, closer disrespected open-close level first. This is a **quantified version of Video 12's "prefer the closer/more recent disrespected level" refinement** — now with an actual number (70%) instead of just a qualitative preference.

## STOP-LOSS PLACEMENT — mechanized rules (direct answer to student question, very concrete)

### Origin trades
Place stop **just beyond the strong high/low** (the sweep+break origin point) **plus extra room to breathe**. This is the safer/more conservative placement, used specifically for origin trades where the structural point is the true invalidation level.

### Continuation trades
Personal preference: a **fixed ~10 pips** stop (more aggressive/"adventurous") rather than the full structural distance, because a wider structural stop can take too long to reach 1:3, reducing capital efficiency/trade frequency. Explicit tradeoff acknowledged: tighter stops get hit more often by liquidity-hunting ("sniper stop-loss") moves before the real move happens.

### Padding rule (minimal, concrete)
"Put your stop loss above the high (or below the low), add one more pip (or half a pip)" — a small fixed buffer beyond the reference point, not a percentage or ATR-based buffer.

### Real trade admission (honest risk disclosure)
Describes a real trade where a tightened stop (~5 pips, moved in from a safer ~7 pips) nearly got stopped out before running to 1:3 anyway — explicit acknowledgment that tight stops carry real risk of premature exit even when the underlying thesis is correct.

### Nuance from student's own trade (important judgment call)
A student's tight stop above a nearby MINOR key level got stopped out; mentor's guidance: sometimes it's safer to place the stop above a more distant, MAJOR structural high (the actual A-shaped key level) rather than a closer minor level, since minor levels get liquidity-hunted more easily. **Not a fixed rule** — requires judgment about which nearby level is more likely to be swept as inducement before the real move.
