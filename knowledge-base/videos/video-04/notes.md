## Core Framework: "Advanced Market Structure"

The market always does exactly **two things** (repeated as the central mental model):
1. **Attack liquidity** — sweep a resting pool of stops/orders
2. **Rebalance an imbalance** — fill/mitigate a fair value gap (FVG)

Every trade idea is built by identifying: *where is the liquidity price needs to attack, and where is the imbalance price needs to rebalance — and at what specific price level, inside that imbalance, do we expect price to react?*

## Internal Range Liquidity (IRL) vs External Range Liquidity (ERL)
- **Internal Range Liquidity (IRL)**: minor/inducement liquidity resting *inside* a defined range (small swing highs/lows between the range extremes).
- **External Range Liquidity (ERL)**: liquidity resting at the *extremes* of a defined range (the major swing high/low that defines the range).
- Expected price behavior (repeating cycle): price sweeps IRL first, then expands to sweep ERL. After ERL is taken, expect reversal; the move that follows becomes the next range's origin.

## The core setup ("if I traded one setup for life, this is it")
Two variants, both high-probability, built on the same two conditions (liquidity to attack + imbalance to rebalance):

1. **"Origin of the move" entry** (highest quality, easy to miss):
   - Liquidity sweep, followed immediately by a break of structure (BOS)
   - This confirms the order-flow/bias direction
   - Entry is at a "key level" (a support/resistance-style level, shape described as "V shape killer" or "A shape killer" depending on price reaction shape) that overlaps *inside* a fair value gap / imbalance
   - This is the point where order flow is first confirmed

2. **"Continuation setup" entry** (his stated preferred/most repeatable one, used when the origin move is missed):
   - Find a key level that was previously **disrespected** (broken through)
   - Wait for price to create a new **inducement liquidity** (a small engineered liquidity pool / minor swing point) close to that level
   - That level, overlapping an imbalance, with inducement resting nearby, is the entry trigger
   - Requires patience — wait for confirmation, don't force it

Both setups are described as "highly probable" as long as there's inducement liquidity resting above/below and a key level overlapping an imbalance.

## "Key level" shapes (his terminology)
- **V shape killer**: a V-shaped price reaction/reversal at the key level
- **A shape killer**: an inverse/A-shaped reaction (needs more examples to pin down exact criteria — flagged for follow-up in later videos)

## Trade management
- Primary target: **1:3 risk-reward** ("that's all 1:3 has done for me") — describes this as his bread-and-butter, repeatable target. Occasionally lets winners run further (mentions 1:6 in one example).
- Explicit lesson (from a live trade review): don't abandon a 1:3 already hit hoping for more — a trade that reached 1:3 then reversed and stopped him out was cited as a direct lesson to take the 1:3 and not get greedy.
- Stop-loss sizing example given: ~10 pips on a specific EU/GU setup (not stated as a universal fixed number — appears to be structure-derived, i.e. placed relative to the imbalance/key level, not a fixed pip value).

## Entry confirmation / multi-timeframe approach
- Bias/setup identified on higher timeframes (1H, 4H, even up to 3H used for investigation in one example).
- Actual entry execution drops to lower timeframes: mentions entries on the **15-minute** chart with confirmation on the **5-minute** chart.
- Uses both candlestick AND line charts on the same pair — line chart specifically called out as important for seeing liquidity sweeps / BOS more clearly (candlesticks can obscure a clean break that's obvious on the line chart, due to wicks).

## Session timing rules (concrete, backtestable)
- He is a **day trader**, does not swing trades overnight as a rule (his "model" is explicitly built/tuned for intraday, not swing, though he notes it *can* work for swing).
- **Avoid GBPUSD and EURUSD during the Asian session** — insufficient "liquidity injection" (his term for institutional order flow) in those pairs during Asia.
- **Pairs suitable for Asian session**: AUDUSD, NZD pairs, USDCHF, GBPJPY, EURJPY — pairs correlated to Asian-session-active currencies (AUD, NZD, JPY, CHF).
- Rationale given: London, New York, and Asia each have their own "liquidity injection" time tied to real institutional/interbank order flow (referenced a documentary about interbank FX dealing as the source of this mental model).
- Will delete a pending limit order if its expected trigger time falls outside London/New York session for a majors pair (e.g. GU) rather than let it fill during Asia.
- Avoid holding into very late session hours — mentions spread widening dramatically around 10pm (session close / rollover), a practical execution-cost concern.
