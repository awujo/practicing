## Reconfirmed (no new mechanics, but strong real-world validation)
- Weekly/monthly candle-close-vs-key-level analysis repeatedly and successfully predicts multi-week/multi-month directional continuation in live historical examples (GBPUSD, Gold).
- "Key levels are candle closures" — repeated as a mantra throughout.
- Key levels double as both entries and exit points (reconfirmed again).
- The IRL->ERL / ERL->reversal cycle holds at monthly scale just as cleanly as daily/weekly (several 2-3 month sustained directional examples shown).

## New: cross-asset applicability claim
Explicitly states the same SLK framework applies to **synthetic indices** (Boom/Crash-style) and by extension crypto, not just forex/gold — "it's applicable everywhere." States he doesn't think synthetics need separate backtesting — just apply the same logic. **Not independently verified by us** — worth testing empirically across asset classes rather than assuming it holds, since market microstructure differs meaningfully between forex and synthetic/crypto instruments.

## New: secondary heuristic on news (soft, unverified by him)
Quotes a past mentor ("John Fibonacci"): **"news goes in the direction of the higher timeframe bias"** — i.e. news-driven moves tend to align with, not against, the prevailing higher-timeframe trend. He explicitly states he has never traded news himself and hasn't personally validated this — treat as a secondary/soft heuristic only, not a core rule. Relevant to Video 8's news-event caveat: this doesn't contradict "treat news as elevated risk," it just adds a directional prior if we do model news days.

## Nuance on holding period (reconciles "day trader" claim from Video 4)
Real examples here show the OVERALL BIAS persisting for weeks to months based on weekly/monthly candle closes, with individual trades still entered/exited on shorter timeframes repeatedly in that direction — not one continuous multi-month position. This reconciles with his "I'm a day trader, I don't swing" statement in Video 4: the higher-timeframe bias is a multi-week/month filter, but execution stays intraday/short-term within that filter.
