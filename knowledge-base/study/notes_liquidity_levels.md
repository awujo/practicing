# Kevin King: liquidity, key levels, structuring (coder notes)

## 0. Meta / caveats
- Liquidity transcripts are noisy (liq tail, liq3 first 35s garbled; kl3 ~600-700s garbled). "ILO/IRA/ERL/eye/air" = IL(H/L), "week" = wick, "condo" = candle, "bills/bis" = BMS, "eye" = high, "air" = high, "Bos"=BOS.
- Course is a "storyline/bias" method: structure -> key levels -> liquidity -> entry model (entry model NOT in these videos). Kevin says explicitly "how to use key levels not taught yet" [T kl3@1722].

## 1. Liquidity
1. Definition: liquidity = resting orders (money); price moves toward bigger liquidity pools [T liq@19-60, T kl4@2130-2165]. Not mechanical beyond the types below.
2. Exactly two types: IRL (internal range liquidity) and ERL (external range liquidity) [T liq@269-318].
   - Inside the current swing range = IRL; outside (beyond range extreme) = ERL [T liq@1196-1216].
   - Rule: price takes IRL first, then expands to ERL; after ERL taken, price returns to IRL (retracement), then continues [T liq@373-423, T liq@1500-1560, T liq2@200-231]. After IRL is taken: "reversal pullback" then expansion [T liq@1540-1554].
   - Targets: in a bullish trade TP = ERL (the external high); ERL is a target, not a stop point; price may break it and continue [T liq@1305-1330, 1440-1510]. Entry zone = near the IRL (the reaction at IRL gives entry) [T liq@457-482].
   - Frame: sketch labels IRL on the HL low and ERL on the HH high in uptrend; flip in downtrend [F liq/sheet_015, F liq/sheet_023 shows shaded IRL zone = the pullback leg between HL and the next high, ERL = the prior high line].
3. Sweep vs break (core mechanic):
   - BMS/BOS requires a candle BODY close beyond the last internal high/low (in trend: beyond last HL/LH). A wick-only poke that closes back = SWEEP; the level was "liquidity" [T liq@647-745, 787-801, 1064-1100]. Body close through = level was a "structural point" [T liq@755-790, 881-933].
   - Sweep of IRL low in uptrend and close above => bullish continuation toward ERL; close below => structure shift (price will "fail to create next HH") [T liq@1144-1190].
   - Frame confirms drawing: "sweep" label below the low, "bos" label on high break, "hl" later [F liq/sheet_015].
   - Wick-to-wick sweep with trend = clean continuation; body break against = deeper move [T kl1@168-336].
4. Forms of liquidity (he says he only teaches the ones he uses): (a) single-candle liquidity, (b) trendline liquidity [T liq3@64-110]. Also: protected highs/lows inside a range count as liquidity ("these highs are liquidity because they are protected") [T liq3@915-930]; "inducement" = the lower-high/second-BOS-low formed before price breaks (advanced structure) [T liq3@812-841, T kl1@207-239]. He explicitly does NOT use sessions (Asia hi/lo) "we don't actually treat sessions" [T liq2@438-448]. Equal highs/lows: never mentioned. 
5. Single-candle liquidity (SCL): one candle's high = IRL and its low = ERL (or vice versa) [T liq2@65-100; F liq2/sheet_003 "one candle as both IRL and ERL"]. Direction-dependent:
   - Downtrend (BOS down, then a retrace candle): candle high = IRL, candle low = ERL. Price sweeps/ takes the high (often into a key level), then targets the low [T liq2@127-250, 1150-1180, 1484-1506].
   - Uptrend: candle low = IRL, high = ERL; price sweeps the low, taps daily key level, closes above, then targets the high [T liq3@429-470, 560-590].
   - TF = whichever candle: monthly candle -> previous month high/low; weekly -> PWH/PWL; daily -> PDH/PDL; H4, H1 similarly [T liq2@308-460]. Frame: candle drawn as grey bar with wick, hline at top labelled IRL [F liq2/sheet_003 t=220].
   - Sequence: take IRL (can be several candles, "one, two, third candle cleared it") then ERL. Don't expect 1 candle [T liq2@915-960, liq3@588-629].
   - If price takes IRL and closes THROUGH (body) in trend direction => BOS, trend continues; if it closes back => treated as liquidity grab => look for reversal only if it then closes beyond the last level created [T liq2@960-1010].
   - Counter-trend example: price took high of a bearish candle but failed to take its low because trend was bullish and a daily key level reacted: only trade with trend [T liq3@640-730].
6. Trendline liquidity: in a trend, retracement forms as a trendline; wait for break of that trendline (retracement over) -> price then goes to take IRL then the protected ERL highs; start looking for entries to continue trend [T liq3@783-1027]. Mechanical: need pivot-fitted trendline; break rule not defined (body? wick?) — resolve: use body close.
7. Bias use: bias direction comes from market structure first ("first consider: is market bullish or bearish") [T liq2@1612-1626]; liquidity gives (a) next target, (b) where to expect entry. Never trade against trend ("not advisable to sell in a bullish market") [T liq3@712-723].
8. Sweeps of previous day/week high/low are valid bias builders; the thing that matters is what happens AFTER the sweep (close back vs through) [T liq3@1599-1618].

## 2. Key levels (kl1, kl3, kl4)
Definition: zone where price is expected to react; "decision" zones [T kl1@656-710]. Vocabulary: rejection (price touches level and pushes away forcefully) [T kl1@840-903]; acceptance/failure dropped.
Three base types, all built from TWO consecutive candles; wicks irrelevant, BODY prices matter [T kl1@1219-1241]:
- A-shape: bullish candle then bearish candle whose bodies share the same level (the bull close = bear open, so the peak at the top). Looks like "A" on a line chart. Used for SELL [T kl1@1173-1255, 1884]. 
- V-shape: bearish then bullish candle sharing that price (valley). Used for BUY [T kl1@1255-1280, 1895].
- Open-Close (OC): two consecutive candles of the SAME direction: bullish-bullish (OC used for BUY) or bearish-bearish (OC for SELL). Not visible on line chart [T kl1@1477-1530, 1685-1711]. Frame: OC drawn as two same-colour bars with the zone line between them (close of 1st / open of 2nd) [F kl3/sheet_005: "open close" arrow to two stacked bars]. A and V drawn with horizontal line at the shared body level, bars at top (A) / bottom (V) [F kl3/sheet_005, F kl1/sheet_014].
- Zone price: shared body level (A: close of bull = open of bear ~ peak; V: close of bear = open of bull). For OC: open of candle 2 / close of candle 1 (named "open-close"). Wick-extreme zone is NOT used; no explicit zone height given -> coder choice: line at shared level, zone = overlap of two bodies. Mark this as ambiguity.
- Timeframes: any; HTF levels take priority (H4/D/W) and a level that looks "unfresh" on H1 can be fresh on H4 [T kl4@1272-1360]. Weekly/daily levels preferred for bias [T kl4@1665-1680].
- Direction bias vs trend: in an uptrend most sell-levels (A) get invalidated, only buys respected; vice versa in downtrend [T kl3@985-1160]. "Level that fails" flips.
- Invalidation (disrespect): price closes a FULL BODY impulsively through the level (A: above; V: below; OC likewise: bearish OC invalid when a candle closes full body above its open/close; bullish OC when closes below) [T kl3@296-349, 1514-1580]. After invalidation, the level flips: invalid A -> expect retest then BUY; invalid V -> retest then SELL; invalid bearish OC -> buy on retest; bullish OC -> sell [T kl3@349-420, 700-760, 1536-1580]. Kevin: "flip zone / breaker block". A level may flip repeatedly [T kl3@1612-1671].
- Respect: price taps in and closes back (rejection) [T kl1@840].
- Fresh vs unfresh (kl4):
  - Fresh = created, not used. Used (unfresh) = a WICK has traded into/from it. A full-body candle through it does NOT use it; stays fresh [T kl4@441-590, 640-690]. Frame: single candle bars + line, "fresh/unfresh" sketch; wick poking into level line [F kl4/sheet_010 t=720-780].
  - Unfresh -> fresh again when a later full-body candle closes through it [T kl4@694-716, 796-805, 1519-1550 (a flipped A becomes a fresh V)].
  - Count only the first wick touch as one "use"; further wicks while unfresh don't count [T kl4@960-1010].
  - Priority: fresh levels > unfresh when picking candidates; he uses a level ONCE (after used, look for another). Others use 2-3 times; no standard; make it a config (default 1) [T liq3@1258-1330, kl4@1087-1110, 1665-1690].
  - Fresh status is judged per-timeframe; weekly may be fresh while H1 shows many uses [T kl4@1699-1735].
  - Pt of confusion: some transcript "fresh/unfresh" strings are ASR-collapsed ("a Fresh Key level is a level that has been created and has also been used" = unfresh).
  - Exception: unfresh level still tradable with "criteria" (e.g., sweep of previous day high then closing below the A-shape, "continuation pattern") [T liq3@1450-1560].
- A level reaction alone is not an entry: need a reaction/criteria first ("not blindly place trade around key levels") [T kl4@1240-1260, kl3@1688-1715].

## 3. "Structuring" (struct video, no chart)
- Not technical analysis: it is a trading plan structure with two parts: WHEN (is there a setup/storyline today?) and WHERE (entry/stop location) [T struct@262-345, 480-500].
- WHEN = storyline built from: basic market structure -> advanced market structure -> (liquidity/inducement) -> key levels; all must align; if incomplete, no trade that day [T struct@560-720, 255-260]. Examples of a valid storyline: "weekly rejects weekly level AND daily breakout"; "daily rejects daily + H4 breakout is not a setup, only direction" [T struct@581-607].
- WHERE = entry; if storyline is clear but no entry/where to put SL, skip (can't define stop) [T struct@500-540, 735-760, 919-930].
- Other rules: no correlation shortcuts (USD-weakness => buy all); one clean trade per day/week is enough; max daily loss 0.5% (~$250 on $50k) then stop; 1:4 RR typical [T struct@836-880, 1400-1650]. No numeric thresholds for bias beyond these.
- Bot implication: output NO-TRADE when storyline unclear; bias = HTF structure direction when structure+key-level+liquidity agree.

## 4. Proposed bias algorithm from these videos
1. HTF (W/D/H4) structure via body-close BMS: HH/HL = bull, LH/LL = bear, else range/no-trade [T kl1@440-500, liq@647].
2. Find ERL (unbroken protected extreme beyond current range) and IRL (HL/LH or pullback extreme inside range); target = ERL in trend direction.
3. Sweep detection: wick beyond level + close back = liquidity taken (continuation if with trend); body close through = structure break/flip.
4. Candidate key levels: A/V/OC on HTF; fresh only; in direction of trend (A/bearish-OC sells in downtrend; V/bullish-OC buys in uptrend); flipped levels (invalidated) treat as opposite-direction level after retest.
5. Bias bullish if trend up, price swept IRL low (or reacted from fresh V/bull-OC) and ERL high still unswept; bearish mirror; no-trade if counter to HTF trend or storyline unresolved.

## 5. Ambiguities for the coder
- Exact zone box for A/V/OC (line vs overlap of bodies); transcript says "body, same level" only.
- "Impulsive": defined only as a full-body close through the level (no size threshold).
- Trendline liquidity break criterion; trendline fitting method.
- Equal highs/lows, session levels: not covered (explicitly session ignored).
- "Used" counting: only wick touch; no proximity tolerance (pips) given.
- How many times a level may be reused: personal 1, config.
- Contradiction: kl3@1646 says all key levels remain valid after repeated disrespect vs kl4 fresh-priority; reconcile as priority ordering, not exclusion.
- Contradiction: liq part: "IRL taken -> ERL"; also "after ERL taken, price looks for external range" (typo for internal) [T liq@403-415]; use the corrected rule.
