# Bishop Supplementary: "10min Part 1 - Bias & Entries"

## Content type
Defi Bishop (independent student of the same mentor) breaks down 4 real
trades from the prior week (GBPAUD, Japan225, US30, EURGBP) end-to-end
from bias to entry. Strong independent cross-validation of the core
mentor framework rather than new mechanics — same terminology, same
cascade, same core rules, applied live to real trades with real (mixed)
outcomes including a stop-out.

## Cross-validates the exact mentor cascade (independent confirmation)
Every trade follows the identical process already documented in Sections
2 and 5:
1. Daily rejects daily key level ("daily reject daily") -> sets bias.
2. 4H gives a **breakout** in that direction.
3. A **"single candle liquidity"** forms — Bishop's own term for exactly
   the mentor's "Single Candle Formation" (SCF) concept from Video 18
   (one candle serving as IRL/ERL). This is a genuinely valuable
   **independent cross-validation**: Bishop is a different student, and
   arrived at/uses the identical single-candle liquidity concept
   without apparent direct reference to Video 18's specific framing —
   strong evidence this is a real, stable part of the shared mentor
   framework rather than an idiosyncratic one-off explanation.
4. Price sweeps that single-candle liquidity and reacts from a nearby
   key level (closest one preferred, consistent with the
   disrespected-level-priority / QMR proximity rules in Section 5).
5. Drop to 1H for confirmation entry — variously a **breakout**, an
   **engulfing**, or a **sweep+break**, all treated as valid confirmation
   triggers (consistent with Section 5's confirmation-entry patterns).
6. Target: the **nearest external range liquidity (ERL)** — stated
   explicitly as Bishop's personal target-placement preference ("that's
   personally how I place my target"), directly matching Section 6's
   tiered take-profit refinement (entry-timeframe ERL as the first
   natural target).

## Reconfirmed core rule, stated in Bishop's own words
"Once the external liquidity is taken, expect a reversal" — verbatim
restatement of Section 3/4's IRL/ERL reversal-vs-continuation rule,
independently arrived at.

## Personal-style variation (minor, explicitly flagged as personal)
Bishop states he does NOT always wait for full candle-close confirmation
before acting ("personally, I don't exactly wait for the candle to
close, but that is personal... you can decide to wait or not"). This is
a deviation from the mentor's emphasized candle-closure discipline
(Section 4's wick-vs-close rule, ~90% reliability figure) — but Bishop
explicitly frames it as a personal stylistic choice, not a framework
rule change. Consistent with Section 5's already-documented
Aggressive-vs-Conservative entry-style spectrum (this is effectively an
aggressive-style variant applied to confirmation timing specifically,
not just entry timing).

## Risk-management honesty (real trade outcomes, not cherry-picked)
- One entry (Japan225) got "stopped out badly" on an engulfing
  confirmation entry — explicitly stated this did NOT mean the bias was
  wrong, just that the individual trade didn't work out. Directly
  reinforces the "market has a mind of its own" probabilistic framing
  already in STRATEGY.md (Videos 12/13).
- One entry (EURGBP) reached its target zone but did NOT get the
  expected reversal/continuation the way GBPAUD/Japan225/US30 did.
  Explicit lesson: **"because it did not happen here doesn't mean you
  should have exaggerated targets... there's always tomorrow."** A
  genuinely useful risk-management/expectation-setting note: don't widen
  targets speculatively just because the pattern worked on other recent
  trades — treat each setup on its own terms.
- One trade (GBPAUD) had its FIRST planned entry never trigger (price
  didn't return to the first key level) — had to wait for a second
  4H breakout and a fresh setup before actually entering. Reinforces the
  "requires patience, don't force it" guidance already in Section 5B.
