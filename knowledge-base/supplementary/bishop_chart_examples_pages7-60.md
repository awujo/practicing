## NEW: Explicit real-time weekly-vs-daily conflict resolution (AUDUSD
example, pages ~26-27)
A rare example includes actual written trader reasoning (not just chart
labels): **"Weekly recent breakout bearish, ERL's standing"** /
**"Daily recent breakout was bullish, Looking for shorts to the
liquidity, More confidence since weekly is bearish."**
- This is a concrete, explicit confirmation that when Weekly and Daily
  conflict, **Weekly wins** — the same priority relationship already
  documented between Daily and 4H (Section 2's "Daily beats 4H" rule),
  now explicitly confirmed one level up (Weekly beats Daily) with the
  trader's own reasoning in writing, not just inferred from mechanics.
- The daily-direction move against the weekly bias is treated as a
  liquidity draw/inducement to fade, not a genuine reversal — directly
  consistent with the "induced-move trap" already in Section 2.
- **Backtester implication**: the priority-cascade rule (Section 2)
  should be implemented as a strict, uniform hierarchy at every level —
  Weekly > Daily > 4H > lower timeframes — not just the Daily > 4H
  relationship that was most explicitly stated in the core course.

## Reconfirmed terminology / labeling conventions
- **"PDH/PDL swept"** (Previous Daily High/Low swept) used as standard
  shorthand liquidity-reference label throughout (Silver, GBPNZD, GBPJPY,
  EURGBP, AUDCAD, GBPCAD) — confirms the abbreviation convention
  worth using directly in code/data schemas. "Old daily low swept" used
  once as an equivalent alternate phrasing (EURJPY).
- **"H4 rejects H4 at extreme"** (USOIL example) — confirms the
  "extreme" key-level terminology (Section 5) applies at the H4 level
  too, not just daily/weekly.
- Consistent teal/pink shading convention reconfirmed across every
  example: teal = expected continuation/target zone, pink = the
  imbalance zone being tapped/filled before continuation.
