# Bishop Supplementary: "10min Part 2 - What Next" (FINAL Bishop video)

## Content type
The sixth and final Bishop supplementary video, covering real trades on
GBPCHF, GBPCAD, BTC, EURGBP, and USOIL. Almost entirely reinforcement of
the already-documented cascade — no fundamentally new mechanics — but
several valuable cross-validations that close out the supplementary
material cleanly.

## Reconfirmed: "QMR key level" used by Bishop too
Bishop explicitly uses the term directly ("this QMR right here, this QMR
key level, which was very close to the liquidity"), independently
confirming the term is shared mentor/community terminology, not isolated
to the core course. Consistent with — though not adding new precision
beyond — Video 17's resolution (Section 5/9): a key level tested/
respected multiple times before disrespect, here also described as
sitting close to the relevant liquidity pool.

## Reinforced: breakout alone can be sufficient confirmation, without
waiting for a subsequent liquidity sweep
Explicit statement: "it's not all the time that you have to wait for
price to come back and take a liquidity. This is what confirms your
direction." I.e., a clean breakout by itself can be enough to commit to
the directional bias and start looking for entries, without always
requiring a full liquidity-sweep-then-reaction sequence first. This
generalizes Video 12's "IRL optional for continuation trades" nuance
(Section 5) — reinforcement, not a new rule.

## Reconfirmed: "high swept, low still standing" confirmation pattern is
mentor-taught, not Bishop-original
The specific confirmation-entry variant already described in the
"Repeating System" video (one side of a liquidity swing point gets
swept, the other side remains standing, producing a valid V/A-shape
entry) is referenced again here as something "introduced by [the
mentor]" (partial transcription garble, but the attribution is clear) —
confirms this pattern is core canonical teaching, not a Bishop-specific
technique layered on top.

  summary of the entire continuation-trading approach — a concise

  counter-weekly-bias trades are a known, named exception category, used
  sparingly and consciously, not a hidden inconsistency in the rules.
