# Bishop Supplementary: "Building Bias Part 1 - Key Levels"

## Content type
The first installment of Bishop's "building bias" two-part series (Part
2, "Building Bias with Liquidity," is already documented in
`b_building_bias_case_study_notes.md`, which previously noted Part 1 was
"referenced but not itself in our video set" — that gap is now closed by
this file). This video is the clearest single explanation of Bishop's
K-Level taxonomy and the core 3-part bias-building cascade, applied to 5
real pairs: AUDJPY, EURUSD, SPX500, BTC, and XAUUSD. Almost entirely
reinforcement of the already-documented mechanics (Sections 2-5), but
gives the cleanest, most explicit statement yet of the K-Level
disrespect/reversal rule and an explicit flexibility caveat for the
3-part cascade.

## Reconfirmed: K-Level taxonomy, stated with unusual precision
- **V-shaped K-Level**: a level where price is expected to react and buy
  (a low-side reaction point).
- **A-shaped K-Level**: a level where price is expected to react and
  sell (a high-side reaction point) — named for its inverted-V/peak
  shape, mirroring the V-shape.
- **Open-close K-Levels** (bullish and bearish variants): a level defined
  by where a candle closed and the next candle opened at the same price
  — functionally the same "engulfed open-close key level" concept
  documented elsewhere, but this video gives the cleanest verbal
  definition of why they're called that.
- **Disrespect/role-reversal rule, stated with full symmetry**: any
  K-Level's default polarity (buy zone or sell zone) inverts the moment
  price closes through it in the "wrong" direction — a V-shaped buy
  level that gets closed below becomes a sell level; an A-shaped sell
  level that gets closed above becomes a buy level; and the same applies
  to both open-close variants. This is the same disrespected-level
  concept already documented in Section 5/9, but this video is the
  clearest single statement of the rule applying uniformly across all
  four K-Level shapes, not just the ones previously illustrated.

## Reconfirmed: the 3-part bias-building cascade (crisp restatement)
Building bias with key levels requires, on the timeframe where the level
lives: (1) a rejection, (2) candle closure confirming the rejection, and
(3) a breakout or breaker structure on the NEXT lower timeframe. Once
this cascade completes at one timeframe pair, the resulting external
liquidity target propagates downward, and the same breakout-or-sweep-
and-break check repeats recursively at each subsequent lower timeframe
pair (weekly->daily->4H) — identical in structure to the recursive
cascade already documented from the Building Bias 2 video and Sections
2-4, now confirmed as explicitly Bishop's Part 1 (key-level) framing
specifically, independent of the Part 2 (liquidity) framing.

## NEW (minor): explicit flexibility caveat when the lower-timeframe
breakout doesn't show up cleanly
If the expected breakout/breaker-structure confirmation does NOT appear
cleanly on the immediate next-lower timeframe, but instead the price
shows one or two candles rejecting off the SAME higher-timeframe level,
Bishop explicitly permits treating that as sufficient confirmation to
start building bias from that lower timeframe instead — provided you
then still require the full breakout/sweep-and-break confirmation on the
NEXT timeframe down from there. Demonstrated directly on EURUSD: the
weekly level didn't get a clean daily breakout, but two daily candles
rejected the same weekly zone, so Bishop treated that as "daily reject
daily" and required the 4H breakout as the next real confirmation gate.
This is the same fallback rule already documented in the plug-and-play
notes (`b_plug_and_play_notes.md`) for a slightly different scenario
(missing daily confirmation after a weekly rejection) — this video
confirms it as a general-purpose cascade fallback, not a plug-and-play-
specific technique, and states it independently of that video.

## Reconfirmed
- "Price doesn't always need to return to the breakout level — the
  breakout/liquidity's job is just to validate direction, not to be
  revisited" — restated explicitly on both AUDJPY and EURUSD ("price
  must not necessarily come to your breakout level... price can even
  just continue buying from there"). Directly reinforces the "IRL
  optional for continuation trades" nuance already documented (Video 12,
  Section 5, and the What Next video).
- Explicit discipline example (BTC): despite daily giving a technically
  bullish breakout, Bishop declined to buy because it wasn't in alignment
  with the weekly/overall bearish structure — reconfirms the strict
  higher-timeframe-bias discipline already documented from the Daily
  Routine video.
- Explicit honest forward-looking hedge on XAUUSD ("I'm not saying these
  buys cannot be valid... if I'm wrong, I'm wrong, I'm just giving my own
  personal view") — consistent with the probabilistic, non-dogmatic
  framing already established throughout the Bishop material.
